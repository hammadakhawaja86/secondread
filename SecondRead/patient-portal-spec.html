Product design specification  ·  Second Opinion Radiology UK

A calm place to send your scan and get an answer

An account-based patient portal that replaces the current enquiry-and-email service. It takes a patient from the first anxious click through upload, consultant matching, expert review and a published second-opinion report — with a radiologist-authored comparison against the report they already have.

01

Product decisions taken up front

These are the calls that shape everything downstream. They are decisions, not options — if any of them is wrong for the business, changing it changes the spec.

Account before upload, not after

Patients create an account at the start of the case, not after paying. Medical images and reports must never sit in an anonymous session that cannot be recovered, and a half-finished case with a real account behind it is a case the clinical team can chase. The account is created with email, a password and a verification link; nothing beyond an email address is asked for before the patient can see the upload screen.

Pay last, upload first

Payment sits at step five, after the images are in and validated. Taking money before knowing whether the imaging is usable creates refunds, and refunds on a health service create complaints. The price is shown from step four onwards and never changes after submission without an explicit conversation.

The patient sees a service, not a workflow

Internally a case moves through triage, allocation, reporting, quality review and release. The patient sees six states. Internal sub-states never leak into patient-facing copy, and no status ever hints at a clinical finding — "Under expert review" means the same thing whether the scan is normal or not.

Nothing clinical leaves the portal

Emails and push notifications carry no findings, no body part, no scan type and no consultant impression. They say a thing has happened and link to a sign-in. This is both a UK GDPR position and a plain human one — nobody should learn something about their health from a lock screen on a train.

Comparison is a clinical artefact, not a diff

The Your report comparison module is authored by the reporting consultant inside the reporting tool. Nothing in it is generated, inferred or auto-matched. Its categories are a fixed clinical vocabulary (§9) so that patients, GPs and the business all read the same word the same way.

Not an emergency service, said once and said well

Emergency guidance appears in exactly three places — the case-detail sidebar, the report viewer footer, and the confirmation screen — in a consistent, non-alarmist form. It is never a red banner and never an interstitial.

Standing emergency copy

This service is not for urgent symptoms. A second opinion takes at least 12 hours and is not a substitute for seeing a doctor. If you feel unwell or your symptoms are getting worse, contact your GP, call NHS 111, or in an emergency call 999.

Sitemap rules

  • One case, one URL. /cases/:id is the spine of the experience. Every notification, every email, every dashboard card resolves to it.
  • The report is a child of the case, not a separate object. A patient who bookmarks the report and returns after sign-in lands on the report, not the dashboard.
  • Deep links survive sign-out. Any portal URL hit while signed out goes to /sign-in?next=… and returns the patient to where they were aiming. The next parameter is validated against an allow-list of portal routes — never an open redirect, and never carrying a case ID into a page title or a browser history entry a shared device can read.
  • No case data in URLs beyond the opaque ID. No scan type, no body area, no name.
  • Support is global, messages are per-case. "How do I download my report?" is /support. "Can you clarify the finding on my L4?" is /cases/:id/messages, because it needs to reach the reporting consultant with the case attached.

Navigation model inside the portal

Desktop uses a persistent top bar with the account menu on the right and no left sidebar — patients have one to three cases, not a workspace, and a sidebar would be mostly empty. Mobile uses the same top bar with the account menu collapsed into a sheet. There is no bottom tab bar; the portal is not used often enough to earn one.

The clock rule

If the clinical team asks the patient a question, the turnaround clock pauses and the case shows Action needed. The patient is told this explicitly at the moment they are asked, so a delay they caused never reads as a service failure — and so the business is not held to a 12-hour promise while waiting on a reply.

04

Exception paths

Each exception is specified as: what triggers it, what the patient sees, what unblocks it, and what the clock does. Every one of them resolves into the dashboard's Action needed queue rather than into an email the patient has to go find.

4.1 Existing patient signs in

Trigger: a returning patient clicks Get a second opinion or Patient dashboard.

Behaviour: they land on the dashboard, not on step 1. If they have a case in flight, the new-case CTA carries a quieter label — Start another case — and the confirmation step re-asks consent for the new case only. Personal details (name, date of birth, contact) are pre-filled from the profile and shown as a read-only summary with an Edit link, so step 1 becomes a five-second confirmation rather than a re-typing exercise.

Clock: unaffected. Cases are independent; a second case does not queue behind the first.

4.2 Incomplete or unsupported upload

Trigger: validation at step 3 finds a file that is not DICOM, PDF or a supported image; a ZIP that unpacks to nothing readable; a study with too few images to be a full series; or a file that fails a virus scan.

Behaviour: the file row turns to an error state in place, alongside the files that succeeded. The patient is never bounced out of the step or shown a modal. Copy names the specific problem and the specific fix (§7.4). If the patient cannot resolve it, Send me my files by post or WeTransfer instead opens a support thread and the case is held as a draft.

Clock: not started. Nothing has been paid for.

4.3 Missing original report

Trigger: the patient reaches step 4 without uploading a radiology report.

Behaviour: not a blocker. A single, plainly worded choice: I don't have the original report — which reveals a short field asking what the patient was told and by whom, plus a checkbox to let the team request the report from their provider on their behalf. The patient is told once, without drama, that the comparison module is only produced when an original report exists.

Inline note
Without the original report, your consultant will still give you a full independent read — but we won't be able to include the side-by-side comparison, because there is nothing to compare against.

Clock: normal. The case proceeds as an independent read.

4.4 Prior scan available for comparison

Trigger: the patient indicates at step 3 that they have earlier imaging of the same body area.

Behaviour: an optional Prior studies upload zone appears, with its own date field per study. Prior imaging is labelled distinctly throughout the case and in the report viewer, and never merged with the current study. The report gains an Interval change line in the comparison module. Copy tells the patient why this is worth the extra effort: Earlier scans let your consultant say whether something has changed, not just what is there now.

Clock: normal. Prior studies do not extend the window.

4.5 Payment failure

Trigger: card declined, 3-D Secure abandoned, or the payment provider errors.

Behaviour: the patient stays on step 5 with everything intact — uploads, answers, consent. The case is saved as a draft immediately on failure, so a closed tab loses nothing. The error distinguishes the three cases, because the fix differs.

Declined
Your bank declined this payment. Nothing has been charged. Try another card, or contact your bank and try again — your case is saved.
3DS dropped
We didn't get confirmation from your bank. Nothing has been charged. Try again and complete the check in your banking app.
Provider error
Something went wrong on our side and the payment didn't go through. Nothing has been charged. Try again in a moment, or contact us and we'll take payment another way.

Clock: not started. The countdown only begins on a cleared payment.

4.6 Clinical team needs clarification

Trigger: the consultant or the clinical coordinator needs information before they can report — a missing sequence, an unclear clinical question, or the date of a symptom.

Behaviour: the case flips to Action needed, the dashboard promotes it to the top, and a task appears with the specific question. The patient replies in the case thread or uploads the missing file directly from the task. The question is written by a clinician but reviewed against a plain-English guide — no abbreviations, no "please provide the axial T2".

Task title
Your consultant needs one more thing
Body
Dr [Name] has started reviewing your scan and needs a little more information before finishing your report. Answering now keeps your report on time.
Clock note
We've paused your delivery time while we wait. It restarts as soon as you reply.

Clock: paused on entry, restarted on reply, and the new expected delivery time is recalculated and shown immediately.

4.7 Report is ready

Trigger: the report passes clinical quality check and is released.

Behaviour: in-app notification, email, and optional SMS — all content-free. The dashboard case card becomes the primary object on the page with a single dominant action, Read my report. First open of the report is timestamped and recorded in the case audit trail; if the report has not been opened in 48 hours, one reminder is sent, and one only.

Clock: stops. Delivery time is replaced by Delivered [date, time], and the follow-up window begins.

4.8 No material difference from the original report

Trigger: the consultant selects no_material_difference as the overall comparison outcome.

Behaviour: this is designed as a good result, not an empty state. The comparison module leads with a full-width reassurance panel above the three columns, and the columns still render, because the value the patient paid for is the independent confirmation itself.

Heading
No material difference identified
Body
Your consultant reviewed your images independently and reached the same conclusions as the original report. That is a meaningful result: two specialists, working separately, agree on what your scan shows.
Next step
Take this report to the doctor looking after you. It supports the plan you have already been given.

Never render this outcome with a neutral-grey empty-state icon, a "no changes found" label, or language that implies the patient wasted their money. The visual weight is the same as any other outcome and the accent colour is the reassuring one.

Push and lock-screen rule

If push or SMS is added, the payload is a fixed, non-clinical string — "Second Opinion Radiology: there's an update on your case. Sign in to view." — with the case reference and everything else fetched only after authentication. No body part, no scan type, no consultant name, no status verb that implies a finding. The same string is used for every event type, so the notification itself carries no signal.

6.3 Responsive behaviour

  • ≥ 1024 px — two columns, 2:1. Right column is not sticky; the page is short enough that stickiness only creates a floating orphan.
  • 768–1023 px — single column. Order becomes: greeting, action needed, active case, start a new case, reports, messages, profile, emergency.
  • < 768 px — single column, full-bleed cards with 20 px gutters. The Start a new case button becomes full-width and moves below the active case card, because a patient opening the portal on a phone is almost always checking an existing case, not starting a new one. The progress timeline rotates to vertical with the current node expanded. Nav collapses to a hamburger; the notification bell stays in the bar.
  • Empty state (no cases) — the greeting is replaced with a single centred block: a one-line explanation of what the service does, the Start a new case button at full weight, and a secondary link to What you'll need to hand. No illustration of a doctor. No empty-box graphic.

What the dashboard deliberately does not have

No percentage progress bar (a number that does not move is worse than a phase name that does), no countdown timer in seconds (it raises the temperature of a page read by anxious people), no thumbnail of the patient's own imaging (a preview of one's own brain MRI on a dashboard is not reassurance), and no marketing modules.

07

New case and upload flow

Five steps, one question-set per screen, with a persistent stepper and a saved draft after every field blur. The patient can leave at any point and come back to exactly where they were, on any device.

7.1 Step structure

StepTitleSub-lineEffort
1About youSo we can label your case correctly and reach you.~40 s
2Your scan and your questionWhat was scanned, and what you want answered.~90 s
3Upload your images and reportThe part that takes longest. We'll wait.2–20 min

7.2 Fields

Step 1 — About you

  • Full name — required. As it appears on your medical records, so we can match your scan to your report.
  • Date of birth — required. Three separate inputs (day / month / year), never a date picker; a picker is hostile for a birth year 60 scrolls back.
  • Mobile number — optional. Only used if we need to reach you urgently about your case.
  • Country of residence — required. Determines VAT treatment and which regulator applies.
  • Who is this for? — required. Myself / Someone I care for. Choosing the second reveals a relationship field and a short consent affirmation. This is asked at step 1 rather than at consent because it changes the pronouns in every subsequent screen and in the report itself.

Step 2 — Your scan and your question

  • Type of scan — required. MRI, CT, X-ray, Ultrasound, PET-CT, Other, I'm not sure. "I'm not sure" is a real, respected answer, not a dead end; it routes to manual triage.
  • Part of the body — required. Brain or spine · Chest or heart · Abdomen or pelvis · Bones or joints · Breast · Head or neck · Whole body · Something else.
  • Date of the scan — required, month and year accepted.
  • Where was it done? — optional. Hospital or clinic name.
  • What would you like your consultant to answer? — required, free text, 600 characters. The single most valuable field in the flow, and the one most likely to be left thin. It gets a large textarea, three example prompts underneath, and no character counter until 500.
  • Relevant symptoms or history — optional, 600 characters.
  • Have you had earlier scans of the same area? — required, yes/no. Drives the prior-studies zone at step 3.
Prompt help
Not sure what to ask? Most people write something like: "Is the lesion in my liver definitely benign?" · "Has the disc problem got worse since last year?" · "Is there anything the first report might have missed?"

Step 3 — Uploads

Three clearly separated zones, in this order and with this emphasis:

  • Your scan images — required. DICOM (.dcm), a DICOM folder, or a ZIP of either. Also accepts the CD/USB folder structure hospitals hand out. Maximum 5 GB per case.
  • Your original report — strongly encouraged. PDF, DOCX, JPG, PNG, HEIC — including a photo of a printed report. Maximum 25 MB per file.
  • Earlier scans — optional, shown only if the patient answered yes at step 2. Each prior study carries its own date field.

7.5 Save and resume

  • A draft is created the moment step 1 is first blurred, and updated on every field blur and every completed upload.
  • Drafts are server-side and tied to the account, so a patient who starts on a laptop and finishes on a phone keeps everything, including uploaded files.
  • A persistent, quiet line sits under the stepper: Saved — you can close this and come back any time. It updates in place rather than appearing as a toast.
  • Drafts appear on the dashboard as a case card in a Draft state with Finish your case as the action, and are the one case type the patient may delete themselves.
  • An unfinished draft triggers one email after 48 hours and one after 7 days. Then nothing. Uploaded images in an abandoned draft are deleted after 30 days, and the email says so.

7.6 Steps 4 and 5

Step 4 — turnaround. Three cards: 48-hour, 24-hour, 12-hour, each with price, the exact delivery date and time that choice produces, and one line on who it suits. The delivery time is computed live from the current moment, so the patient chooses a date, not an abstraction. No card is pre-selected and no card is badged "most popular" — nudging a pricing choice on a health service is the wrong instinct.

Step 5 — consent and payment. Three separate, individually-ticked consents, never bundled: (a) consent for consultant radiologists to access and report on the images, (b) agreement to the terms and privacy notice, (c) an optional consent to retain the images beyond the standard period for future comparison — clearly marked optional and refusable without consequence. Payment is card via a PCI-compliant provider in a hosted field; card details never touch the portal's own DOM.

7.7 Confirmation screen

8.2 Section rules

  1. In short. Consultant-authored, 40–60 words, plain language, always present. This is the first thing on the page because it is the first thing the patient wants. It is never a truncation of the impression — it is written separately.
  2. Your report comparison sits above the full clinical report, because "is this different from what I was told?" is the question the patient paid to have answered. Specified in full in §9. Omitted entirely when no original report was supplied, replaced by a one-line explanation of why.
  3. The full consultant report. Verbatim, in clinical language, in the standard four headings. Not simplified, not annotated, not truncated. This is the document a clinician will read, and altering it would undermine its standing.
  4. What this means for you. The same content in plain English, authored by the reporting consultant, not generated from the clinical text. Reading age target 12. Any term that must stay technical is defined inline on first use.
  5. What we suggest you do next. A short ordered list, always ending with a line that returns the patient to their own doctor. Never phrased as an instruction to stop or change treatment.
  6. Actions. Download PDF (the full report including the comparison and the plain-English section, with a footer carrying the consultant's name, GMC number and the report date). Share with my GP (§8.3). Ask a question (opens the follow-up flow).
  7. Emergency guidance, in the standing form.

8.3 Secure sharing

Sharing sends a link, never an attachment, and never the report itself by email.

  • The patient enters the clinician's name, role and email, and ticks an explicit consent: I'm happy for Second Opinion Radiology to share my report with this person.
  • The recipient gets a content-free email with a link. Opening it requires a one-time code sent to the same address, and the link expires after 30 days.
  • The recipient sees a read-only report with a banner identifying who shared it and when. No dashboard, no other cases, no ability to reply into the patient's message thread.
  • The patient sees a Shared with list on the report with the recipient, the date, whether it has been opened, and a Revoke access control that works immediately.
  • Every share, open and revocation is written to the case audit trail.

8.4 Follow-up

Two options, both time-boxed to 30 days from delivery and both surfaced at the bottom of the report and on the case:

  • Written questions — included. Up to three questions in one thread, answered by the reporting consultant, typically within two working days. Copy is explicit that this is for understanding the report, not a new opinion on new imaging.
  • 15-minute call — paid add-on. Booked against real consultant availability, video or telephone, with a confirmation containing only the time and a join link. The booking screen states plainly that the call explains the report and does not create a doctor–patient relationship or a treatment plan.

9.4 Authoring and safety rules

Non-negotiable

  • No inference. Nothing in this module may be produced by text comparison, diffing, or a model. Every field is typed or selected by the reporting radiologist and is subject to the same clinical sign-off as the report body.
  • Nothing renders before approved_at. A partially authored comparison is not shown in any state, including to the patient's shared clinician.
  • Provenance is always visible. The original column carries "Supplied by you or your imaging provider. We have not altered this text." The second-opinion column carries the consultant's name, subspecialty and GMC number. These labels appear at every breakpoint and in the PDF.
  • Verbatim means verbatim. The original report text is displayed unedited. If it is long, it is truncated with an explicit Show the full original report control — never paraphrased.

9.5 Language rules

The difference between a defensible comparison and a negligence claim is often one verb. These are house rules for consultants and for the UI copy around them.

"Incorrect", "error", "wrong""We read this differently." / "Our interpretation differs."
"The first radiologist failed to…""On our review, we would describe this as…"

Standing footnote beneath every comparison module and in the PDF: Radiology reports can reasonably differ between specialists. This report is an independent opinion based on the images and information provided, and does not replace the advice of the doctor treating you.

12

Accessibility, privacy and clinical safety

Three requirement sets that are acceptance criteria, not aspirations. A build that fails any line here is not shippable.

12.1 Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA

  • Contrast. 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and for the boundary of every interactive control. Status is never carried by colour alone — every chip pairs colour with its own word.
  • Keyboard. Every action reachable and operable by keyboard, in a logical order, with a visible focus ring that meets 3:1 against both the control and the page. The upload zone has a real focusable button, not a drag-only target.
  • Screen readers. Upload progress announced through a polite live region at meaningful intervals, not every percent. Status changes on the dashboard announced once. Errors linked with aria-describedby and moved to on invalid submit. The three comparison columns are a semantic table or a set of headed regions — never three visually adjacent divs with no relationship.
  • Zoom and reflow. Usable at 400% zoom and at 320 px width with no horizontal scrolling of the page body. Wide content scrolls inside its own container.
  • Motion. prefers-reduced-motion honoured everywhere; progress indicators degrade to static text.
  • Cognitive load. One question set per screen. No time limits on any form. No auto-advancing carousels. Session timeout warns at 25 minutes with an extend control, and never discards a draft.
  • Language. Plain English throughout, reading age 12 for patient-facing copy. Clinical text is exempt and is always paired with a plain-English equivalent.

12.2 Privacy — UK GDPR

  • Lawful basis. Contract for the service; Article 9(2)(h) for the special-category health data. Consent is used only where it is genuinely optional — extended retention, marketing, sharing with a third party — and refusing it never blocks the service.
  • Data minimisation. No NHS number, no address, no next of kin, no GP details unless the patient chooses to share the report with them. Nothing is collected "in case it's useful later".
  • Notifications. Content-free, per §5. This is a design requirement and a testable one.
  • Storage and residency. UK or EEA only, encrypted at rest and in transit. Images and reports in object storage with per-object access control; no public URLs; signed URLs expire in minutes, not days.
  • Retention. Stated numerically on the privacy page and echoed in the flow: images and reports retained for eight years by default, abandoned drafts deleted after 30 days, shared links expire after 30 days. Extended retention is the optional consent at step 5.
  • Patient rights, self-serve. /profile/privacy offers a download of everything held, a correction request, a withdrawal of the optional consents, and a deletion request with a plainly stated caveat about the records the business must keep for medico-legal reasons.
  • Audit trail. Every access to a case — by staff, by consultant, by a shared clinician — is logged with actor, action and timestamp, and is disclosable to the patient on request.
  • Access control. Session cookies HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax. Two-factor authentication offered at sign-up and required before a report can be shared externally. Re-authentication required to change email, password or bank details.
  • Shared devices. "Stay signed in" is off by default and labelled Only on a device that's yours. Sign out clears all local state including any cached report.

12.3 Clinical safety — DCB0129 / DCB0160

  • A clinical safety officer owns the hazard log. The statuses, the comparison categories and the emergency guidance are all hazard-log items, and changing their wording is a change that needs re-approval — not a copy tweak.
  • No status implies a finding. "Under review" is used identically for a normal scan and an urgent one.
  • Critical-finding pathway sits outside the portal. If a consultant identifies something requiring urgent action, the clinical team contacts the patient and, with consent, their treating clinician by phone. The portal is never the primary channel for that, and the case simply shows Action needed with a request to call.
  • Nothing patient-facing is model-generated. Not the report, not the plain-English section, not the comparison, not the next steps. Any future assistive tooling operates behind clinical sign-off and is never surfaced as authored content.
  • Identity matching. DICOM header name and date of birth are checked against the account at validation, and a mismatch raises a task rather than silently proceeding.
  • Version integrity. A published report is immutable. A correction is issued as a new version with a visible Amended [date] notice, the reason for amendment, and the previous version retained and accessible.
  • Emergency guidance is not dismissible and appears at the three fixed locations in §1.
13

MVP versus phase two

The MVP is the smallest thing that lets a patient complete a case end-to-end without a human touching the process. Everything that improves the experience but does not gate completion is phase two.

MVP — build first

  • Email + password accounts with verification and password reset
  • Five-step case flow with server-side drafts and resumable chunked upload
  • DICOM, ZIP, PDF and image validation with the error set in §7.4
  • Card payment, with the failure handling in §4.5
  • All six statuses, the dashboard, and the case-detail page
  • Action-needed tasks and the clock-pause behaviour
  • Per-case secure messaging
  • Report viewer with all five content sections
  • Your report comparison, fully consultant-authored — this is the differentiator and cannot wait
  • PDF download
  • Written follow-up questions
  • Content-free email notifications
  • Profile, privacy self-serve, and the audit trail
  • Nav and footer dashboard entry points

Phase two

  • Secure sharing with a GP or treating clinician, with one-time codes and revocation
  • Booked 15-minute consultant calls with live availability
  • An in-browser DICOM viewer with the consultant's key images annotated
  • Two-factor authentication as a default rather than an option
  • Direct image transfer from providers via IEP or an equivalent, removing the CD step
  • Push notifications and SMS on the fixed content-free payload
  • Multiple-patient accounts for carers and parents
  • Saved payment methods and instalments
  • Welsh-language interface and BSL support content
  • Insurer and employer billing routes
  • A returning-patient path that pre-fills a new case from a previous one

Sequencing note

Sharing is the one phase-two item worth arguing about, because "take it to your GP" is the recommended next step in almost every report. It stays out of the MVP only because a downloaded PDF the patient forwards themselves is a workable interim, and because the one-time-code recipient flow carries its own security review. Build it immediately after launch, not eventually.

Open items for the client

  • Payment provider, and whether insurer billing is needed at launch.
  • Confirmed retention period — the eight years above is the common UK adult record standard and needs sign-off.
  • Named clinical safety officer and hazard log owner.
  • Whether "someone I care for" needs formal proof of authority, and what that looks like operationally.
  • Consultant availability data source for phase-two call booking.
  • Whether the current 12-hour turnaround is offered around the clock or within working hours — this changes the delivery-time calculation shown at step 4.
