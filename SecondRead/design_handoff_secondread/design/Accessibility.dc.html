Accessibility

How this site is built to be usable, what we know is not yet good enough, and how to tell us when we have got it wrong.

Last updated 25 August 2026

Our commitment

People come to SecondRead worried, often in pain, and sometimes with the very condition that makes a website hard to use. A site that is difficult to read or impossible to navigate by keyboard is not a minor inconvenience in that situation.

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA across every page, and to go beyond it where meeting the letter of the standard would still leave someone stuck.

What we have done

  • Every page works at 320 px wide and at 400% browser zoom without horizontal scrolling or loss of content.
  • All text meets at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio against its background; large headings meet 3:1.
  • Every interactive control can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone, in a logical order, with a visible focus indicator.
  • Buttons, links and form fields have a touch target of at least 44 × 44 px.
  • Form fields have real, associated labels — never a placeholder standing in for one — and errors are announced, not just coloured red.
  • Images that carry meaning have alternative text. Decorative images are hidden from screen readers.
  • Animation is subdued automatically for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion, and no content moves, flashes or auto-advances in a way that cannot be stopped.
  • The site does not depend on colour alone to convey any piece of information.

What is not there yet

We would rather list these than let you discover them.

  • The sample report is offered as a PDF. It is tagged and readable by a screen reader, but the on-page version above it is the more reliable route, and we intend to make the PDF fully equivalent rather than merely accessible.
  • The consultant carousels on the home page can be operated by keyboard and read in full on the Specialists page, but the carousel itself is a less comfortable experience than the plain list. If you find it awkward, the Specialists page has the same information laid out simply.
  • We have not yet completed an independent audit by a third-party accessibility specialist. One is planned, and we will publish the findings here — including the ones that do not flatter us.

If something does not work for you

Tell us, and we will fix it. You do not need to know what is wrong technically — describing what you were trying to do and what happened is enough.

Email info@secondread.co.uk or call 01438 904272, 09:00–20:00, seven days. We aim to reply within two working days.

If any part of this site stops you ordering a report, call us and we will take the whole order over the phone at the same price. That is not a workaround we are proud of, but it should always be available to you.

Enforcement

If you are unhappy with how we have responded to an accessibility complaint, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service (EASS) at equalityadvisoryservice.com. We are a private-sector provider, so the public-sector accessibility regulations do not apply to us — but the Equality Act 2010 does, and so does the ordinary expectation that a health service should be usable.
